## 1. Technical Hurdles & Failure Modes Encountered
- **Coupled Bench Drag & Mechanical Equilibrium in Quadrant IV:** When driven by an external prime mover applying $T_L = -T_{nom} = -20.602\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$, the rotor accelerates into hypersynchronous speeds ($n > 1800\text{ rpm}$). Viscous bench drag opposes the forward prime mover motion ($B\omega_m \approx +8.19\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ at $192.3\text{ rad/s}$), yielding a net opposing torque on the electromagnetic rotor of $T_e = T_L + B\omega_m = -20.602 + 8.192 = -12.410\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$. Both circuit theory and dynamic simulation converged onto this exact value within $0.008\%$ error.
- **Hypersynchronous Speed Crossing & Negative Slip Dynamics:** Applying the negative mechanical torque disturbance at $t = 5.0\text{ s}$ accelerates the machine from no-load motor speed ($1775.5\text{ rpm}$) past synchronous speed ($n_s = 1800\text{ rpm}$) to settle at $n_{ss} = 1836.42\text{ rpm}$. Slip inverts sign ($s = -0.0202 < 0$), causing the equivalent rotor resistance $R_2'/s < 0$ to become negative, transforming the induction machine into an electrical active power generator.
- **Power Flow Inversion vs. Reactive Excitation Demand:** Simulated instantaneous power verified that active power reverses from $+1578.8\text{ W}$ (no-load motor demand) to $-2218.1\text{ W}$ (active electrical power injected back into the 220 V AC grid). Crucially, inductive reactive power remains positive throughout ($Q \approx +3118.4\text{ var}$), validating that an induction generator cannot self-excite and strictly requires continuous reactive magnetizing power from the external AC grid.
- **Energy Conservation Validation:** Total prime mover mechanical input power ($P_{in,mech} = 3962.0\text{ W}$) exactly equals viscous drag dissipation ($1575.5\text{ W}$) plus copper losses ($168.4\text{ W}$) plus electrical power delivered to the grid ($2218.1\text{ W}$), confirming $100.0\%$ conservation.
